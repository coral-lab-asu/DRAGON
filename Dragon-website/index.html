DRAGON : A Benchmark for Evidence-Grounded Visual Reasoning over Diagrams

About

Diagram question answering (DQA) requires models to interpret structured visual representations such as charts, maps, infographics, circuit schematics, and scientific diagrams. Recent vision-language models (VLMs) often achieve high answer accuracy on these tasks, yet correct answers do not guarantee that models ground their reasoning in the diagram regions that support the prediction. Models may instead rely on textual correlations or dataset artifacts without identifying the visual evidence required to verify the answer.

We introduce DRAGON, a benchmark for evaluating evidence-grounded visual reasoning in diagrams. Given a diagram, a question, and the correct answer, a model must predict bounding boxes that correspond to the visual elements required to justify the answer. These evidence regions may include answer-bearing components, textual labels, legends, axes, connectors, and other supporting structures involved in the reasoning process.

Our evaluation on recent VLMs reveals that even Claude Opus 4.6 achieves a Grounding IoU of only 23.8% on AI2D, showing that faithful visual grounding remains an open challenge. DRAGON supports future research on models that ground their predictions in visual evidence.

Dataset scope (high level): 11,664 annotated question instances from six diagram QA datasets (ChartQA, Circuit-VQA, InfographicsVQA, MapIQ, MapWise, and AI2D), with a 2,445-instance test set carrying human-verified evidence annotations and a standardized evaluation framework.

Dataset

DRAGON is a human-verified benchmark for evaluating evidence-grounded reasoning in diagrams, built from six established diagram QA datasets: AI2D, ChartQA, Circuit-VQA, InfographicsVQA, MapIQ, and MapWise, covering scientific diagrams, charts, circuit schematics, infographics, and geographic maps. The annotation pipeline produces 11,664 annotated question instances, and we release a test split of 2,445 human-verified instances with a standardized evaluation framework (training and validation splits will follow in future work).

Annotation & Verification

Candidate regions are initialized from existing annotations or, when none exist, via template-based region transfer (homography alignment from an annotated template). Multimodal models then propose question-conditioned evidence in the DIAGRAMS interface, and annotators refine it to the minimal set of regions required to justify the answer. Six annotators followed a shared protocol with two independent reviews per instance and senior arbitration. Agreement is measured on 100 instances per dataset under Complete Visual Reasoning (CVR) and Core Evidence Alignment (CEA).

Evaluation Pipeline

Given a diagram, a question, and its verified answer, models must predict bounding boxes for the visual evidence that justifies the answer. We compare three prompting protocols: EDGE predicts the evidence set directly, SAGE first lists the evidence elements and then grounds each one, and VERGE adds a self-verification pass that fixes boxes and adds missing evidence. Predictions are scored with Max-Pair IoU (best single-box overlap), Grounding IoU (overlap of the full predicted and gold evidence regions), and box-level F1.

Qualitative Analysis

We present qualitative grounding results across all eight models and three prompting strategies, illustrating characteristic success and failure modes across both model families and prompt designs.

Model-wise success (MapWise). The gold evidence marks Madhya Pradesh as the state in the 4000-5000 thousand tonnes range and the corresponding legend segment. Closed-source models produce compact, well-localized predictions, while open-weight models exhibit varying degrees of over-prediction and spatial imprecision.

Model-wise failure (ChartQA). The gold evidence marks the favorable (64) and unfavorable (28) data points at 1985, whose difference yields 36. All eight models fail to localize both points, instead grounding chart titles, subtitles, full trend lines, or axis regions.

Prompt-wise success across all six domains. Rows show the ground truth (Original) and predictions under EDGE, SAGE, and VERGE. All three strategies localize the core evidence, with VERGE producing the most complete coverage in most cases.

Prompt-wise failure across all six domains. All three strategies fail to recover the complete gold evidence, with failure modes that persist regardless of prompt design: over-prediction in structurally dense diagrams, missing evidence in multi-hop instances, and imprecise localization in spatially ambiguous regions.
